# Design brief — fill-in template

Fill in the 14 dimensions in order, top to bottom. Do not move past ② target audience until it's
settled — every later dimension ripples out from it (see `target-driven-design.md`). Each
dimension carries one "AI direction example."

## ① Purpose

State, in one sentence, the behavior this design should increase. Pick one: sign-ups, purchases,
inquiries, or applications.

> AI direction example: "The purpose is to increase free-trial sign-ups. Always place a CTA at the
> end of every section."

## ② Target audience (the most important dimension)

Write persona, situation, and emotional state as one set. Age and gender alone aren't enough —
include what state the person is in right now and what they're feeling.

> AI direction example: "The target is a sole proprietor in their early 30s. They're worn out from
> spending too much time on bookkeeping and want reassuring, plain language over technical jargon."

## ③ Impression to avoid

Write this paired with the impression you want. Be specific about what "not to look like."

> AI direction example: "We want trustworthiness, but avoid looking like a stiff, corporate
> enterprise. Don't make the palette feel cold or sterile."

## ④ Information architecture (the order information is revealed)

Write, in order, what gets said first, where anxieties get addressed, and where the call to action
comes.

> AI direction example: "Lead with the service's value in one line, follow with empathy for the
> user's pain point, then the solution, and close with a sign-up CTA."

## ⑤ Page structure

Choose only the sections you need from: Header / Hero / Problem / Solution / Features / Steps /
Testimonials / Pricing / FAQ / CTA / Footer.

> AI direction example: "Use Header, Hero, Problem, Solution, three Feature items, Steps, FAQ, CTA,
> and Footer."

## ⑥ Layout

Pick from 1-column / 2-column / card-based / Bento UI (a grid that mixes large and small cards).
This can vary by screen width.

> AI direction example: "1-column on mobile; 2-column only for the key sections on desktop. Show
> features as cards, 3 columns on desktop and 1 column on mobile."

## ⑦ Visual style (specify as a combination)

Pick around two styles from `style-catalog.md` and combine them — a combination communicates
intent better than a single style alone.

> AI direction example: "Base this on minimal × card-based UI. Keep decoration restrained and let
> whitespace carry the refined feel."

## ⑧ Tone and manner

Combine two or three words describing the overall mood, and state the impression to avoid
alongside them.

> AI direction example: "Natural and approachable, with a sense of trustworthiness. Avoid anything
> that reads as childish."

## ⑨ Color roles

Assign colors by role — primary, background, accent, text — and note the reasoning behind the
impression, not just the color name.

> AI direction example: "Primary is a muted terracotta, background is ivory, and the accent is the
> green associated with the service. Avoid strong black-and-white contrast."

## ⑩ Typography

Specify the size difference between headings and body text, line height, and the impression the
typeface should carry.

> AI direction example: "Headings should be slightly bold and strong; body text at least 16px with
> generous line height. The typeface should feel soft and easy to read."

## ⑪ Components / UI states

Specify the core components (buttons, cards, forms, etc.) and the states they need — hover,
disabled, loading, error.

> AI direction example: "Define the primary button, card, and input form, each with default, hover,
> pressed, and error states."

## ⑫ Spacing, radius, and shadow

Use the amount of whitespace, the corner radius, and the shadow strength to tune softness or a
premium feel.

> AI direction example: "Use generous whitespace, large card radii, and light shadows. Avoid a
> harsh, rigid look."

## ⑬ Responsive design

Specify the approach at each screen width.

> AI direction example: "Collapse to 1 column on mobile, with buttons tall enough to tap easily.
> Never allow horizontal scrolling."

## ⑭ Accessibility

Specify contrast, text size, keyboard operability, and never relying on color alone to convey
information.

> AI direction example: "Ensure sufficient contrast between text and background. Error states must
> show a specific message, not just a color change."
